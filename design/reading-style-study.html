Before the conference · 4-minute guide

What should I actually prioritize?

You can't do everything at a conference. Here's a simpler way to choose what's worth your time—without chasing every big name.

01

Start with what you're there for.

Are you there to find an opportunity, learn something new, meet your people, or share a project? The right choice changes depending on your answer.

A popular event isn't automatically the right event for you.

02

Look for a real reason to go.

Before adding a company, talk or meetup to your schedule, ask yourself:

  • What specifically would I learn or accomplish there?
  • Can I actually access it?
  • What would I be giving up to attend?
A small shift that makes a difference

Instead of “I'll visit the biggest employer because everyone else is going,” try identifying a role, team or technical question that would make a conversation worth your time.

03

See how this changes for your goal.

Choose what you're attending for. The example changes, but the decision-making method stays the same.

Career example: Compare a relevant current job posting with a popular booth that has no verified opening. The employer's official application route comes first; a conversation is additional, not a substitute.
04

Choose a first move—and a backup.

Less useful
“Visit five companies and network more.”
More useful
“Verify one relevant role, prepare a real question, and save one alternative session.”
Show the exact research steps
  1. Open the current conference's official employer, session or community list.
  2. Choose up to two options aligned with your goal.
  3. For a job, open the employer's current posting and confirm eligibility. For a session or event, check the organizer's access rules and schedule.
  4. Record the source URL, what is confirmed, what still needs checking, and one specific question or action.
  5. Keep one useful alternative if your first option isn't accessible.

These steps are general advice, not confirmation that a specific event, job or meeting exists.
