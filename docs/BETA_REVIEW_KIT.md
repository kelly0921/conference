# Small beta review: content and planning usefulness

Use the latest development preview linked in the PR. This is an invitation script and feedback form to copy manually; no invitations have been sent and no responses are collected by the app. Do not use production for the new beta workflow until it is promoted in a reviewed release.

## Who to invite

Start with five to eight willing conference attendees. Include a first-time attendee and people attending for learning, community, career exploration and sharing a project. Include an online or hybrid attendee and someone outside tech. These are useful perspectives, not a statistically representative sample.

## Invitation text

> I’m testing a conference planning resource and would value your candid feedback. Could you spend about 15–20 minutes trying it with a conference you’re considering? The main question is whether it helps you decide what to do next. You can use invented details and skip anything private. AI is optional. Your plan stays in your browser; please share observations rather than your complete plan. There’s no need to send screenshots, financial details, contacts or confirmation links.

Before a session, explain browser-local storage and backups. Ask permission before observing the screen or recording anything. Do not collect passwords, contacts, private invitations or full exports. No recording is necessary. Use an anonymous reviewer code in notes.

## First task — show this without further instructions

> Think of a conference you’re considering or have recently attended. Use the Playbook to make a plan that would help you. When you’re ready, show or describe the plan and tell me your next action. Use invented details anywhere you prefer.

Allow about five minutes for the first attempt, but record actual time rather than marking a slower attempt as failure. Do not first tell the person which button to click or show the example. If stuck, ask “What would you expect to happen here?” Record the point where help became necessary. Then help them continue.

## Observe and ask afterward

1. What did you think this resource would help you do? Did it seem intended for someone like you?
2. Was it clear where to begin? Where did you hesitate, need an example or feel asked for too much?
3. What is the specific outcome you want? What priority did you choose, what will you do next, and what is your backup?
4. What information still needs checking before you commit time or money? Does any saved status or checkmark feel more certain than it should?
5. Open a relevant guide and the complete learning example. Which detail helps you act? Which is obvious, repetitive, unclear or missing?
6. If a follow-up fits your situation, choose a message example or explain why no message is needed. Does it help you say something specific and true?
7. Read the field plan. Is there enough to use during the event? What would you remove or add?
8. What did you expect to happen to your notes after closing the tab, changing browser or clearing browser data?
9. Would you return to this for a real event? What would need to change first?

## Optional, separate AI task

Ask whether the reviewer wants to test AI; declining is a complete and valid choice. Explain that their approved request goes to OpenAI. Use invented/public details and have the person review the exact sharing preview and consent themselves.

Compare their plan before and after the suggestions: did AI make the next action more specific, preserve the goal and limits, or introduce an unsupported claim? Ask them to point to what each suggestion relies on and what remains unknown. A quoted input is not independent verification. The access question supplied by the planner must not be credited as model reasoning. Record a short, nonprivate paraphrase of a problem; collect exact request/output text only with separate explicit permission.

## Feedback form — copy for each reviewer

```text
Reviewer code:
Attendee experience: first conference / attended before / prefer not to say
Main purpose: learning / community / careers / sharing a project / another purpose
Format: in person / online / hybrid

In one sentence, what did you think the Playbook was for?
Where did you begin, and where did you get stuck?
Could you identify a useful outcome, one priority, a next action and a backup?
What still needed checking before making a commitment?
Which guide/example helped most? What was unclear or missing?
Was the field plan usable? What would you change?
Was it clear where your notes were saved and how to keep a backup?
Did the follow-up examples fit, or was no message needed?
Optional AI: skipped / helped / unclear / introduced a problem
Most important improvement:
Would you use this for your next event? Why or why not?
```

Do not put participant names or identifying plans into Git. Keep any consented private notes outside the repository. Share an anonymized synthesis with the team.

## How to review the findings

For each anonymous session, record: completed independently / completed with help / could not complete; the blocking step; whether the final next action was concrete; and the missing or misleading content. Separate content problems, navigation problems, storage misunderstandings and AI errors. Also distinguish missing evidence from a confirmed wrong claim.

Fix any advice that could lead to a wrong access, spending or deadline decision first. Then address repeated confusion and weak examples. Keep contradictory feedback rather than averaging it into a score. Re-test changed tasks with fresh willing attendees. Do not infer broad adoption or AI accuracy from a handful of beta sessions.
